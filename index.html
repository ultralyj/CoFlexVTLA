CoFlex-VTLA: Compressing Spatiotemporal Tactile History for VLA-Guided Stiffness Adaptation in Contact-Rich Manipulation

Yijie Luo1,2,*, Guanghui Qin1,*, Wei Wang1,2, Xingyu Li1,2, Zhipeng Wang1,2, Yanmin Zhou1,2,†, Bin He1,2

1Tongji University  ·  2Shanghai Research Institute for Intelligent Autonomous Systems
* Equal contribution  ·  † Corresponding author

Abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have shown strong generalization in robotic manipulation, yet their predominantly kinematic action spaces remain insufficient for contact-rich tasks that require continuous adaptation to evolving physical interactions. We present CoFlex-VTLA, a tactile-enhanced VLA framework that couples temporal contact grounding with contact-conditioned directional compliance adaptation. CoFlex-VTLA introduces Hierarchical Spatio-Temporal Tactile Modulation (HiST-TM) to capture spatial correlations and multi-rate temporal dynamics from distributed fingertip forces, providing temporally grounded physical context for policy reasoning. Conditioned on the resulting multimodal representation, the policy jointly predicts reference action chunks and Asymmetric Directional Compliance Fields (ADCF), enabling distinct mechanical responses across Cartesian directions and their opposite polarities. The predicted motion and compliance are executed through a high-rate tactile-guided variable impedance controller. Experiments on six real-world contact-rich laboratory tasks show that CoFlex-VTLA achieves a 70.0% overall stage-level success rate, outperforming the strongest baseline by 27.5 percentage points, while improving physical interaction consistency and generalizing to unseen object geometries and sizes without fine-tuning.

Method

CoFlex-VTLA couples temporal contact grounding with contact-conditioned directional compliance adaptation. High-frequency tactile history is first encoded by HiST-TM to ground the policy in the evolving contact state. The grounded multimodal context is then used to jointly predict reference actions and asymmetric directional compliance, which are executed through a high-rate tactile-guided variable impedance controller.

01 Hierarchical Spatio-Temporal Tactile Modulation

HiST-TM models spatial correlations and multi-rate tactile histories to modulate the current tactile representation. A GATv2 network captures spatial correlations among fingertip sensing points per frame. Multi-rate historical sequences at different temporal sampling intervals are progressively integrated through a two-stage S4D encoder. The resulting history summary modulates the current feature via FiLM-style conditioning, and bidirectional cross-attention fuses it with the VLA representation.

02 Asymmetric Directional Compliance Fields

Each compliance command parameterizes independent stiffness along the positive and negative directions of the three translational axes, allowing different mechanical responses along opposite directions of the same axis:

c = [kx+, kx−, ky+, ky−, kz+, kz−]

The effective stiffness along each axis is selected according to the polarity of the end-effector displacement relative to the nominal reference.

03 High-Rate Tactile-Guided Compliance Control

Predicted motion–compliance chunks are executed by a variable impedance controller at 200 Hz. Fingertip tactile measurements are continuously aggregated into a Cartesian contact-force estimate that adjusts the nominal reference:

xv = xr − Gτ K−1 clip(fτ − fref)

Identical force deviations produce larger corrections along compliant directions and smaller corrections along stiff directions, closing the high-rate physical interaction loop.

Tasks

Six chemistry-inspired laboratory tasks covering diverse contact and compliance requirements, grouped into grasp-dominant, wrench-dominant, and twist-dominant categories with 2–3 interaction stages per task.

Ablation Study
